The Untold Story: Black Cherry Creek

Long before luxury boutiques and brunch, Cherry Creek was home to a thriving Black community — built on flood-prone land next to the city dump, the only place segregation allowed Black families to buy property. Their story has been largely erased from the neighborhood's marketed identity.

🏘️

Land Nobody Else Wanted

South of 1st Avenue, the land flooded, smelled of the city dump, and was passed over by white Denver. For Black families excluded by racial covenants, that affordability meant a rare path to homeownership.

👥

The Families

The Jacksons, Scotts, Morrisons, and Robinsons. Nancelia Jackson (1926). Judge Gary Jackson, Warren Scott, Trudi Morrison, Cleo Parker Robinson. Names preserved through oral histories on CPR and Denverite.

🌬️

Environmental Inequality

Smoke and ash from the dump, rats, repeated floods, and the stigma of "undesirable" land — Black residents bore the environmental burden long before the term "environmental injustice" existed.

🛒

Erasure by Renewal

As Sears, the mall, road expansions, and rezoning drove land values up, families were bought out or priced out. By the time the modern shopping district took shape, the Black community was almost entirely displaced.

Key Themes

🌊

Water & Flooding

Cherry Creek's story is inseparable from water — the devastating 1864 flash flood, the 1933 Castlewood Dam failure, and the construction of Cherry Creek Dam to tame the creek once and for all.

🏕️

Indigenous Heritage

The Arapaho (Hinono'ei), Cheyenne (Tsistsistas), and Ute (Nuche) peoples — among 48+ tribal nations tied to this land — used Cherry Creek for hunting, agriculture, and trade. The 1851 Fort Laramie Treaty was violated by gold-rush settlement.

⛏️

Gold Rush Origins

Placer gold discovered in 1858 drew thousands to the confluence of Cherry Creek and the South Platte — creating the rival towns that merged into Denver.

🛍️

Commerce & Development

From the 1953 shopping center to the 1990 enclosed mall to the 2024 Cherry Creek West redevelopment, the area has been a premier retail destination for seven decades.

🚴

Recreation & Trails

Colorado's first state park (1959) and the 40+ mile Cherry Creek Trail connect downtown Denver to Franktown, following ancient Native American paths.

🏙️

Urban Design

Speer Boulevard's 1906 construction channelized the creek. Over a century later, a 2024 vision study proposes restoring biodiversity and improving pedestrian access.

Life in the Era

Period photographs from the Library of Congress capturing daily life across the decades that shaped Cherry Creek. These images are illustrative of each era — not photographs of Cherry Creek itself.

1905 — City Beautiful Era

Fifth Avenue after a snowstorm, New York. The same City Beautiful aesthetic shaped Speer Boulevard along Cherry Creek (1906–1918).

Detroit Publishing Co., 1905. LOC item 2016804564. Public Domain.

1905 — Street Life & Fashion

Up Fifth Avenue from 28th Street, NYC. Long coats, hats, horse-drawn carriages — the world Harman residents would have recognized just before annexation.

Detroit Publishing Co., 1905. LOC item 2016804655. Public Domain.

1941 — Easter Sunday Best

Boys on Easter morning, Southside Chicago. The dignity of dress in Black communities of the era — a parallel to the Sunday-best worn by Cherry Creek's Black families heading to church.

Russell Lee, April 1941. LOC FSA/OWI. Public Domain.

1939 — Saturday Night, Juke Joint

Jitterbugging near Clarksdale, Mississippi. Music, dance, and community — the cultural fabric of Black American life during Cherry Creek's mid-century years.

Marion Post Wolcott, Nov 1939. LOC FSA/OWI. Public Domain.

1957 — The Shopping Era

A supermarket in 1957 — the same year Cherry Creek Shopping Center had been open four years. Postwar consumer culture was reshaping American neighborhoods.

U.S. News & World Report Collection, 1957. LOC item 2017657527. No known restrictions.

c. 1980s — The Modern Mall

An indoor shopping mall from the era when Cherry Creek's enclosed mall was being planned (dedicated 1990) — atriums, fountains, and aspirational retail defined the moment.

Carol M. Highsmith, c. 1980–1990. LOC Highsmith Archive, item 2011635991. No known restrictions.

Where to Dig Deeper

Archives, museums, and oral history collections preserving the full story

📚

Denver Public Library

Western History & Special Collections holds Cherry Creek neighborhood photos, Sanborn fire insurance maps, the 1864 flood images, and the 1920s dump photos. Search "Harman," "Cherry Creek flood 1864," "Garfield Street."

🏛️

History Colorado

Exhibits on Indigenous Colorado, Sand Creek, and Denver's urban growth. Manuscript collections, urban-renewal files, and educational materials connecting Cherry Creek, Four Mile, and Indigenous histories.

🏚️

Four Mile Historic Park

The 1859 Brantner-built stage stop sits in the Cherry Creek corridor. Increasingly interprets Indigenous presence through land acknowledgments, tipi exhibits, and signage about Ute, Arapaho, and Cheyenne peoples.

✊🏿

Black American West Museum

Family photographs and oral histories of Black Denver, including Cherry Creek ties — the Parker Brothers, broader context on Black migration, segregation, and property ownership in Colorado.

🎙️

CPR & Denverite Oral Histories

"Legacy of Cherry Creek: Growing Up Black in Denver's Transforming Neighborhood" — interviews with Judge Gary Jackson, Warren Scott, Trudi Morrison, and Cleo Parker Robinson.

🪶

Tribal Historic Preservation

For Indigenous-centered narratives: Northern Arapaho, Southern Cheyenne and Arapaho, and Ute tribal historic preservation offices, plus oral histories from universities and History Colorado.
